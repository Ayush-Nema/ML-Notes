One-Way ANOVA & the F-test

Do three or more groups have the same population mean? Edit the data below and watch every step update.

1. The big idea: signal vs. noise

ANOVA compares two kinds of variability:

  • Between-group variability (signal): how far the group means are from the overall (grand) mean.
  • Within-group variability (noise): how spread out the values are inside each group.
F = MSB / MSW = (between-group variance) / (within-group variance)

If the group means really are equal, F should be near 1. If F is large, the means are far apart compared with the natural spread, which is evidence that at least one mean differs.

2. Hypotheses and formulas

H0: μ1 = μ2 = … = μk (all group means are equal)
H1: at least one mean is different

SSB = Σ nj (x̄j − x̄)2    dfbetween = k − 1
SSW = ΣΣ (xij − x̄j)2    dfwithin = N − k
MSB = SSB / (k − 1)    MSW = SSW / (N − k)
F = MSB / MSW

k = number of groups, N = total number of observations, x̄j = mean of group j, x̄ = grand mean.

Assumptions and cautions

  • Observations are independent.
  • Each group is roughly normally distributed.
  • Groups have roughly equal variances.
  • A significant F says some difference exists, not which groups differ. Use a follow-up test (e.g., Tukey's HSD) for that.
  • F is never negative, and the F-distribution is right-skewed, so we only look at the upper tail.
