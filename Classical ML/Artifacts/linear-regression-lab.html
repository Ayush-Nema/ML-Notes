What the line does—and its limits

It summarizes a straight-line pattern and predicts y from x. A pattern alone does not prove that changing x causes a change in y. Many other factors may affect exam scores.

Use predictions near the observed data. Outside that range, the pattern may not continue. A zero slope means no fitted linear trend; curved relationships can still exist.

Why squares? What exactly is minimized?

Each vertical error is actual score − predicted score: eᵢ = yᵢ − (b₀ + b₁xᵢ). Add the squares: SSE = Σeᵢ². Squaring prevents positive and negative errors from cancelling and gives large mistakes more weight. The shaded squares use the vertical-error length as their side in score units; their areas are proportional to eᵢ².

Ordinary least squares with an intercept calculates b₁ = Σ[(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)] / Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² and b₀ = ȳ − b₁x̄. This finds the minimum directly; the computer need not try every line. When all x values are equal, a unique slope and intercept cannot be identified.

A / FIND THE COEFFICIENTS

How does OLS get this line?

Intuition: start at the center of the data, (x̄, ȳ). Ask whether students above the average in x also tend to be above the average in y. Divide this joint movement by the spread in x to get a slope. Then choose the intercept so the line passes through the center.

1. Find the averages
x̄ = Σxᵢ / n   ·   ȳ = Σyᵢ / n
2. Center each point and add its contributions
Sxx = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²
Sxy = Σ[(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)]

A positive product means x and y deviate from their averages in the same direction; a negative product means opposite directions. Positive and negative products may cancel.

Every student's contribution to the OLS calculation
Studentxᵢyᵢxᵢ − x̄yᵢ − ȳ(xᵢ − x̄)²(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)
3. Calculate the slope
b₁ = Sxy / Sxx
4. Calculate the intercept
b₀ = ȳ − b₁x̄

These formulas minimize SSE for a straight line with an intercept. They calculate the solution directly. If all x values are equal, Sxx = 0 and the slope and intercept are not uniquely determined.

The baseline for R²
Predict ȳ for every student. Its total squared error is SST = Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)².

How to compare: smaller SSE, MSE, RMSE, and MAE mean smaller errors on the same data. OLS minimizes SSE (and therefore MSE and RMSE on these fixed data), but does not necessarily minimize MAE. SSE increases with dataset size, so do not compare raw SSE across datasets of different sizes.

Here, MSE divides by n to average prediction errors. The residual variance estimator SSE/(n − 2) is a different quantity for simple OLS. These metrics evaluate the data used here; they do not measure performance on unseen students. R² is not a measure of causation.
